# Solar Photovoltaics

Solar photovoltaics (PV) convert sunlight directly into electricity using semiconductor materials, most commonly silicon. When photons strike the cell, they excite electrons, creating a direct current that an inverter converts to alternating current for the grid or local use.

## Key Technologies
- **Monocrystalline silicon** — highest efficiency (~20–24%), higher cost.
- **Polycrystalline silicon** — slightly lower efficiency, cheaper to produce.
- **Thin-film (CdTe, CIGS)** — flexible, lightweight, lower efficiency but good for large utility-scale farms.
- **Perovskite cells** — emerging technology with rapidly rising efficiency and low manufacturing cost.

## Role in the Energy System
Solar is the fastest-growing renewable source globally. Its intermittency (no output at night, reduced output under cloud) makes it tightly coupled to [[Energy Storage]] and [[Smart Grid]] infrastructure. Rooftop solar also feeds directly into [[Smart Buildings]] in urban environments.

## Challenges
- Land use for utility-scale farms.
- Recycling of end-of-life panels.
- Grid integration and the "duck curve" effect on evening demand.
