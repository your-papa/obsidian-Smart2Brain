# Wind Power

Wind power harnesses kinetic energy from moving air to spin turbines that drive generators. It is one of the cheapest sources of new electricity in many regions and scales from single small turbines to vast offshore farms.

## Onshore vs. Offshore
- **Onshore wind** — cheaper to install, but faces siting, noise, and visual-impact concerns.
- **Offshore wind** — stronger, more consistent winds and larger turbines, but higher capital and maintenance costs.

## Technology Trends
- Larger rotor diameters and taller towers capture more energy.
- Floating offshore platforms open deep-water sites.
- Digital monitoring via [[IoT Sensors]] enables predictive maintenance.

## System Integration
Like [[Solar Photovoltaics]], wind is variable. Forecasting and storage are essential, linking wind closely to [[Energy Storage]] and [[Grid Integration Challenges]]. Wind also pairs well with [[Green Hydrogen]] production during surplus periods.
