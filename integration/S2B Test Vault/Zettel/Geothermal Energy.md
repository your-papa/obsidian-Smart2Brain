# Geothermal Energy

Geothermal energy taps heat stored beneath the Earth's surface to generate electricity or provide direct heating. Unlike [[Solar Photovoltaics]] and [[Wind Power]], it is available 24/7, making it a valuable baseload renewable source.

## Technologies
- **Hydrothermal** — uses naturally occurring hot water or steam reservoirs.
- **Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS)** — engineered reservoirs in hot dry rock, expanding viable locations.
- **Ground-source heat pumps** — shallow geothermal for heating and cooling [[Smart Buildings]].

## Advantages
- Constant, predictable output with a high capacity factor.
- Small land footprint compared to solar or wind farms.
- Long plant lifetimes.

## Limitations
- Geographically constrained to tectonically active regions.
- High upfront drilling cost and exploration risk.
- Induced seismicity concerns with EGS.
